About

I am a chemical engineer and MSc researcher at BUET working in process systems engineering. My interest is in using mathematical models to make better decisions about how chemical processes are designed, operated and made safe.

My MSc research focuses on solar-driven zero liquid discharge desalination. I developed a transient, plant-level model of a closed-loop, multi-plate falling-film solar still, used global sensitivity analysis to identify the parameters that most influence its energy demand, and am now working on surrogate-assisted optimization of its design.

I also work as a research assistant on an industry-sponsored project on the reaction kinetics of ammonia synthesis over a liquid-metal catalyst, where I design and plan the experimental programme needed to obtain kinetic data. Earlier, in my undergraduate work, I applied Bayesian networks to gas pipeline risk in Dhaka and optimized a sulfuric acid converter.

I am applying to PhD programs in process systems engineering.

Research projects

2025 – now

Zero liquid discharge desalination

Freshwater scarcity is pushing more regions toward desalination, yet conventional plants return a concentrated brine that is costly to dispose of and harms coastal and inland ecosystems. Zero liquid discharge removes that waste stream by recovering nearly all of the water, but it is energy-intensive, which limits its use where power is expensive or unreliable. Solar-driven systems offer a route to make it practical, provided their energy demand is well understood and controlled.

The objective of this work is to understand and reduce the energy demand of a closed-loop, multi-plate falling-film solar still. I developed a transient, plant-level model in MATLAB that captures the coupled heat and mass transfer through the whole system, including the closed air loop, feed preheating and an air-to-air recuperator. I used the model to evaluate the recuperator's effect on energy consumption, applied global sensitivity analysis to identify which design and operating parameters matter most, and located the operating limits that constrain how much water the system can recover.

Funded by the Deanship of Scientific Research, King Faisal University.

2026 – now

Reaction kinetics of ammonia synthesis over a liquid-metal catalyst

Ammonia is essential for fertiliser and food production, but the Haber-Bosch process that makes almost all of it depends on solid catalysts, high temperatures and pressures, and very large, energy-intensive plants. Reaction heat is hard to remove from a packed solid catalyst bed, and mass transfer inside it limits how efficiently the catalyst is used, which keeps capital and operating costs high. A lower-cost alternative would make ammonia more accessible, particularly at smaller scale.

This project replaces the conventional solid catalyst with a liquid-metal catalyst, whose fluid nature is expected to improve both mass transfer and heat transfer and so make lower-cost production possible. Because no intrinsic kinetics have been published for this system, they must be measured experimentally. To make that possible I developed the piping and instrumentation diagram (P&ID) for the experimental rig and carried out a HAZOP analysis to identify and manage its process hazards. I am now planning equipment procurement, after which I will build the complete setup and run the experiments that provide the kinetic data. The work is an industry collaboration with Amrark Ltd. under Dr. Noor Mohammad, together with researchers at UC Berkeley.

Funded by Amrark Ltd.

2023 – 2024

Bayesian network model of urban gas pipeline failure

Natural gas is central to Bangladesh's energy system, supplying homes, industry and power generation, and in Dhaka it is delivered through a dense, ageing distribution network running beneath crowded neighbourhoods. Leaks in such a network can lead to fires and explosions with serious consequences for people and property. Managing this risk normally starts from historical failure data, but Bangladesh has no systematic record of pipeline incidents and operational losses, so conventional statistical methods cannot be applied.

I built a noisy-OR Bayesian network in GeNIe to estimate leak frequency in Dhaka's distribution network despite this data gap. Instead of failure statistics, the model draws on the knowledge of gas-sector practitioners, whose judgements were elicited and converted into model parameters. It links the main causes of leakage, such as corrosion, third-party damage and equipment failure, to the likelihood of a leak, giving operators a structured, transparent way to quantify risk. This supports prioritising inspection and maintenance on the most vulnerable parts of the network, and the model can be updated as real incident data are collected. I also used sensitivity analysis to identify the most influential failure mechanisms.

2023 – 2024

Design of a 150 tonne-per-day sulfuric acid plant

Team lead, undergraduate process design project, BUET

I led a four-member team through the full process design of a double-contact sulfuric acid plant, from market feasibility to economic analysis. The work covered the process flow and piping and instrumentation diagrams, material and energy balances, equipment sizing and hazard analysis, and involved working with industry professionals and equipment vendors.

My individual contribution was the four-bed SO₂ catalytic converter. I built a one-dimensional packed-bed reactor model, sized the beds, and used constrained nonlinear optimization (Calderbank kinetics, SQP) to minimise the catalyst inventory, reducing total catalyst weight by 18.88% (17,588 to 14,268 kg). I also completed the converter's mechanical design as a pressure vessel to ASME Section VIII.
